Introduction

Our Honeymoon
The Idea

Sake, sakura, and someone to share it with

Unlike many couples who spend their honeymoon on a beach or relaxing at a resort, we decided to spend 17 days exploring some of Japan's most beautiful and culturally rich regions.

From the biggest city in the world to small villages in the countryside, and a few things in between, we wanted to document our trip highlights and share them with our loved ones!

Chapter One
京都

Kyoto

The Old Capital
Arrival

Lantern light and temple nights

From the moment we stepped into our AirBnB to tatami mats, wooden beams, and a zen garden, we knew we were in for a culture shock - though exactly how great of one was unexpected. Kyoto was surreal, and exactly like what you would expect of a cultural capital.

Each day we made a point to trek to at least one different shrine or temple, culminating in trips to both Fushimi Inari-taisha (a large hill, whose trails are encapsulated by over 10,000 torii gates) as well as the bamboo forests of Arashiyama, where a local sakura festival was in full swing!

Uji

A quick trip to the matcha capital

With a love of matcha to be satisfied, we decided to take a small trip (~30 minutes by train) to Uji, the matcha capital of Japan! Needless to say, we returned home with a carry-on's worth of matcha, hojicha, and tea cakes.

"Yeah - this might be the best thing I've ever tasted." - Carole, one bite into a sakura and black sesame matcha affogato.

Unfortunately, we were unable to get reservations at the Nintendo Museum there, but after some tea and snacks, we were able to visit Byodoin temple - an absolutely gorgeous buddhist temple and museum whose central gilded sanctuary was dubbed "The Phoenix Hall".

Kii Mountains

Enter the forest. Leave the noise

In order to psych ourselves up to tackle the largest city in the world, we decided to get in sometime away from any people, hustle, and bustle. The Kumano Kodo is a UNESCO recognized cultural pilgrimage across the Kii peninsula, traveled by nobles who wished to cleanse their being of their past and present selves. As it happens, the Kumano region is recognized as a place where one dies and comes back to life anew.

So we traveled south along the coast to our lodgings in the (western) coastal town of Kii-Tanabe, making sure to stop by the cultural heritage center to receive our UNESCO passports to stamp at shrines along the way, and set to rest.

We were no strangers to strenuous hikes, though this - backpacking through a foreign land, with next to no understanding of the language and customs - would be a first for both of us.

⊹ ⊹ ⊹
Day One
熊野古道 一

Hosshinmon-oji to Yunomine

Villages & Shrines
Into the Forest

The first steps on a thousand-year path

Starting from Kii-Tanabe, we took an early morning bus with nothing but our packs to the nearby shrine of Hosshinmon-oji, where we would begin our trek. During this leg of the hike, the terrain was largely still rural, though lived in; the first half of this led in and out of forests and small neighborhoods, allowing locals to leave produce, crafts, and refreshments in small farmstands for pilgrims to purchase.

"When building Yunomine onsen, a carpenter's apprentice would bring him lunch every day from Hongu, giving some in offering at the Jizo shrine near Yunomine pass. The carpenter, noticing the missing food, sought to catch him in the act; upon doing so, he struck the apprentice across the nose. Instead of the apprentice being harmed, however, the nose of the Jizo fell from the statue. The Jizo repayed the apprentice for his daily offering" - Kumano folklore

Midway through, the trail leads through Hongu, a very small town home to Kumano Hongu-tasiha and its torii - the largest torii gate in all the world - and some of the most incredible and affordable sushi and udon to fuel us for the rest of our day. We continued onward, gaining more and more elevation until we began our descent into Yunomine Onsen to soak our aches in natural hot springs.

Day Two
熊野古道 二

Ukegawa to Koguchi

Mist & Mountain
The Long Climb

Where the trail gets serious

Fully rested, we were ready for the hardest leg of the hike. Leaving the beauty of Yunomine was no small feat, and this day's journey threatened tough terrain and rain (that luckily came after we settled into town). Most of the day was spent hiking through dense forest, stopping at small shrines along the way, though occasionally the foliage broke, and we were able to look out from some high vistas.

This day's journey ended in Koguchi, a small agricultural village with one small convenience store, a cafe, an abandoned elementary school, and a campground. Near the campground, two guys had set up a food truck, and were selling souvenirs brought back from Bolivia! We hung around for a while before walking back into the sleepy village, to the former co-op building turned lodgings where we would spend the night. Of all the places that we visited, we both agree that Koguchi was a serene, special place.

⊹ ⊹ ⊹
Day Three
熊野古道 三

Koguchi to Nachi Taisha

Down to the waterfall
The Final Descent

Nachi Falls, and the end of the path

Before dawn, we began a steep climb up from Koguchi - the first third or so of the day would prove the most challenging. The misty, dense forests along this climb then made way to the ruins of abandoned tea houses, built to accommodate travelers and pilgrims alike. Unlike the first day (which felt like hiking through communities) or the second (which was invariably just "trees"), the third showed us numerous different environments, the aftermath of a landslide, and even some incredible wildlife (a gorgeous pheasant and a tanuki)!

By early afternoon, we were arriving at Nachi-taisha, the nation's first official Shinto-Buddhist fusion temple, and the end of our journey. We ate a late lunch and celebrated with ice cream before exploring more of the grounds. This shrine is nestled into the side of a valley, and is at the gateway to Yoshino Kumano National Park, the broader park system through which we were largely hiking. From the park's visitors center, we hopped on a bus that would take us to Kii-Katsura, a fishing village where we'd be spending the night.

⊹ ⊹ ⊹
Chapter Three
紀伊勝浦

Kii-Katsuura

Tuna & More Tuna
Sea Air

The freshest tuna we'll ever eat

We arrived in Kii-Katsura just before sunset, so we checked into our gorgeous accommodations and sought dinner - when in fishing village on the Pacific coast of Japan, what else could you want other than sushi? We located a local sushi counter, joining some familiar fellow pilgrims, new friends ranging from the Netherlands to the faraway reaches of Littleton Colorado (a mere 20 minutes from our own home), and reveled in our triumph while reflecting on our journey with a solemn reverence.

"It really doesn't get much fresher than this, does it?" - Ryan

We made sure to wake up bright and early after the most violent thunderstorms either of us had ever witnessed (even having lived through numerous hurricanes) so that we could witness Kii-Katsura's main attraction - a tuna market's daily auction of the night's haul! Observing how these tuna were unloaded, assessed, and arrayed based on buyer was fascinating, though the true treat came immediately after. Next to the auction was a food hall, where restaurants would buy the daily catch and serve it for breakfast. We opted for "Mochi Tuna" rice bowls that were quite possibly the best tuna dishes we'll ever stand to have.

⊹ ⊹ ⊹
Chapter Four
大阪

Osaka

City lights & theme parks
After the Quiet

Neon, noise, and street food

From Kii-Katsura, we took another train, this time to Osaka - a city known for its food and nightlife. We explored many of the busier areas of Dotonbori and were exposed to our new favorite meal (sukiyaki), though the crowds and Vegas-style lights and marquis proved fairly overstimulating.

We were also able to enjoy Osaka Castle before peak times (though still incredibly busy), and ambled around its surrounding parks before making our way to Osaka's pottery row, where locals set up shop with handmade wares.

任天堂

Universal Studios Japan

Before leaving Osaka, we had to visit our very first international theme park - Universal Studios Japan! This experience was equal parts familiar and foreign, like a member of your extended family you haven't seen in years; we both knew what to expect, but also were able to be surprised when we least expected it! Many of the rides were similar, though the inclusion of dedicated Peanuts and Pokemon areas/attractions were welcomed additions by Carole and Ryan, respectively. Just before leaving, on a whim, we decided to get in line for a roller coaster themed as "Club Zedd", which ended up being the collective favorite of the day. Who knew Clarity would still be such a banger 14 years later?

Chapter Five · Final
東京

Tokyo

A City of Villages
Finale

Not one city — many

Tokyo was too large and too varied to be accurately penned here. Despite being the most populous city metro in the world, so much land is covered that at no point did Tokyo feel overcrowded at all. So many different neighborhoods and pockets were drastically different from each other that any time we stepped above ground from the subway, it felt like we might as well have been in a new city. And there was ALWAYS something going on.

"Why can't 7-Eleven be this good at home?!" - Carole, after her 5th morning konbini smoothie.

We stumbled into countless sakura festivals, and even tried to go thrifting but ended up in a local craft beer fest. The food was absolutely insane, and despite the variety, we ate Tsukemen that was so ridiculously good, cheap, and fast that we ended up going back the next day. We spent about six days in Tokyo, and I truly am not sure that we experienced enough of it to effectively communicate the scale and breadth of potential.

美術

teamLab Planets

teamLab is a collective of artists with many immersive exhibits around Japan, though their Planets exhibit was something to behold. Half of it was a high-concept art installation, and the other half an interactive playground for kids; while that may have created a somewhat muddied venn diagram with casualties, it still managed to take our breath away, where we could sit on the floor, staring up at the ceiling for twenty minutes at a time. The experience was capped off with a spontaneous walk-into a secluded and empty bar, though it was certainly advertised. For how insanely cool this sensory-deprevationesque sake bar pod was, I'm shocked that we didn't have to wait hours.

ディズニー

Tokyo DisneySea

It wouldn't be a vacation with Ryan O'Hara without a trip to the closest Disney. We ended our long journey in a place of familiarity, but somehow still unlike anything we've experienced before. This park is probably the most un-Disney like park we've ever experienced. Despite it being the rainiest, windiest, day on our trip, we were able to enjoy this unique place and only want to check out Tokyo Disneyland even more.
